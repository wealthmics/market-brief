Commodities · Silver
Silver Pulls Back, but Is the Bigger Story Still Intact?
Silver has witnessed an extraordinary rally over the past year, significantly outperforming both gold and copper. However, the sharp correction following its January 2026 peak has raised questions about the sustainability of the rally, particularly as expectations around industrial demand begin to change.
Why Has Silver Corrected After Such a Strong Rally?
As shown in the chart, silver prices surged nearly 300% from their December 2024 baseline at their peak, considerably outperforming gold and copper. However, the subsequent reversal reflects a shift in market sentiment, with profit booking after the steep rally and concerns around the sustainability of industrial demand adding to selling pressure.
Source: Bloomberg
Is Solar Demand Becoming a Headwind for Silver?
Solar has been one of the fastest-growing sources of industrial demand for silver, with photovoltaic demand rising from 82.8 million ounces in 2021 to 186.6 million ounces in 2025. However, demand is forecast to moderate to 151 million ounces in 2026, reflecting a shift in the industry’s consumption pattern rather than a collapse in the broader solar growth story.
Source: World Silver Survey 2026, The Silver Institute (Metals Focus)
How Solar Manufacturers Are Reducing Silver Use
Solar manufacturers are increasingly improving efficiency and reducing the amount of silver used per cell through silver thrifting. The main technological alternative is copper, particularly copper based metallisation, which is cheaper and more abundant than silver. Manufacturers are also reducing silver usage through thinner silver contacts and advanced cell designs, such as TOPCon and heterojunction technologies. This could limit the pace of silver demand growth even as solar installations continue to expand. However, solar remains an important part of silver’s long-term demand story alongside electronics, electrification and other industrial applications.
Where Could Silver Prices Head Next?
Silver has already rebounded from around $49/oz to above $51, showing that buying interest remains strong after the recent correction. The area around $49 is particularly important, as it is close to the previous 2011 peak and could now act as a key level for the market. If silver can hold and consolidate above this area, the recent correction may prove to be a temporary pause rather than the beginning of a deeper decline.
Analyst consensus: $66.75/oz average silver price forecast for 2027 | Source: Reuters poll, October 2026
If selling pressure continues, the monthly gap near $40 highlighted on the chart becomes an important level to watch. The next move will depend largely on investment flows, bond yields, the US dollar and industrial demand.
Looking beyond the recent volatility, the correction could offer a good opportunity for long term investors to gradually accumulate silver, supported by both its investment appeal and growing industrial use.
Why It Matters
Silver’s recent correction highlights its higher volatility compared with gold, driven by its dual role as a precious metal and an industrial commodity. While near term volatility may continue, the correction could offer a more attractive entry point for long term investors, supported by demand from electronics, electrification and renewable energy. The focus should remain on long term allocation rather than chasing short term price moves.
